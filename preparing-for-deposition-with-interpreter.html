Deposition Interpreter Preparation: A Practical Guide

A practical preparation guide for attorneys, witnesses, and legal teams working with an interpreter during a deposition.

6 min readPractical Client GuideDavila & Associates, Inc.

Start with the right interpreter

A deposition is already a precise, on-the-record process. When an interpreter is involved, preparation helps counsel, the witness, the court reporter, and the interpreter work from the same structure and reduces avoidable interruptions.

Schedule the interpreter as early as possible and provide the language and, when relevant, the regional dialect. If the matter involves highly technical terminology or an uncommon language combination, additional lead time can be especially helpful.

Before schedulingConfirm the language needed, deposition date and time, expected duration, location or remote platform, and whether any special credentials are required by the court, agency, or parties.

Provide useful context before the deposition

An interpreter does not need to know the merits of the case, but appropriate context can improve readiness. When permitted, provide information such as the general subject matter, names that may be difficult to pronounce, specialized terminology, acronyms, and exhibits or documents likely to be referenced.

For remote depositions, send the platform information and access instructions ahead of time. A brief technology check can prevent audio, microphone, and connection issues from becoming part of the record.

Use a clear speaking rhythm

During interpreted testimony, clarity is more important than speed. Attorneys and witnesses should speak in complete, manageable segments and avoid talking over one another. Long, multi-part questions can be difficult to interpret accurately without pauses.

  • Speak directly to the witness, not to the interpreter.
  • Use first person: “Where were you?” rather than “Ask her where she was.”
  • Pause after a complete thought so the interpreter can render it accurately.
  • Allow the interpreter to request repetition or clarification when necessary.
  • Avoid side conversations while testimony is being interpreted.

Protect the clarity of the record

The interpreter’s role is to facilitate communication faithfully and neutrally. If there is confusion over a word, phrase, or interpretation, resolve it on the record rather than allowing multiple people to speak at once.

If an attorney needs to discuss a matter privately with the interpreter or witness, follow the same procedures you would use for any other off-the-record or privileged discussion.

Deposition interpreter checklist
Confirm language and dialect.
Reserve the interpreter early.
Provide date, time, location or remote link, and expected duration.
Share appropriate terminology, names, and reference materials in advance.
Confirm audio and technology for remote proceedings.
Ask speakers to use one voice at a time and manageable sentence lengths.
