What Makes a Translation “Certified”?

Understand what “certified” commonly means, how it differs from notarization, and what information to confirm before you submit an official document.

5 min readPractical Client GuideDavila & Associates, Inc.

What “certified translation” usually means

In the United States, the phrase certified translation commonly refers to a translation accompanied by a signed certification or statement attesting that the translation is complete and accurate to the best of the translator’s or language-service provider’s ability.

It does not automatically mean that a government agency has issued a universal “certified translator” license. Requirements can vary by the organization receiving the document, so the safest approach is always to confirm the recipient’s instructions before ordering the translation.

Important distinctionA certified translation and a notarized translation are not the same thing. Notarization typically verifies the identity/signature of the person signing a statement; it does not independently verify the accuracy of the translation.

Documents that often require certification

Certified translations are frequently requested for official or administrative purposes. Examples can include:

  • Birth, marriage, divorce, and death certificates
  • Academic transcripts and diplomas
  • Immigration and identity documents
  • Legal records and court filings
  • Business, licensing, and regulatory documents
  • Medical records when submitted for an official purpose

What should be included?

The exact format depends on the recipient, but a certification generally identifies the translated document and includes a statement regarding completeness and accuracy, along with the name/signature of the certifying person or organization and the date.

If a court, government agency, university, employer, or foreign authority provides its own wording or formatting requirement, send those instructions with the source document.

Ask these questions before ordering

Certified translation intake checklist
Who will receive the translation?
Does the recipient specifically require certification?
Is notarization, an affidavit, or another authentication step required?
Does the recipient require a specific layout, seal, signature, or delivery format?
What is the deadline and will an electronic copy be accepted?

Send the complete document

For the most accurate quote and translation, provide clear copies of every page—including stamps, seals, handwritten notes, signatures, and reverse sides when they contain information. Cropped or incomplete scans can delay the project because the translator may need to request missing content.
