Plan the Spanish version

Audience, tone and terminology belong in the brief.

Tell us whether the document is for Spanish-speaking readers in the United States or a specific country. Regional vocabulary, formality and organization-specific language affect how a message is understood.

  • Describe the readers and their location.
  • Provide your glossary and approved Spanish references.
  • Identify product names and terms that should stay unchanged.
  • Specify the final file format and intended distribution.
  • Flag character limits, form fields or layout constraints.

Spanish wording can take a different amount of space than English. Reviewing the format early helps identify layout needs before delivery.

Ongoing organizational projects

Keep recurring communication consistent.

For handbooks, policies or a series of related forms, start with the current approved English version. Send prior translations as reference and tell us which terms your team has already approved.

When the source changes, identify the revised sections and the version that replaces the earlier file. A designated reviewer can help resolve terminology questions and prevent conflicting instructions across departments.

Frequently asked questions

Planning an English-to-Spanish project.

Which variety of Spanish should our organization request?

Tell us where your readers are located and how the material will be used. Spanish for a U.S. audience may call for different vocabulary or phrasing than content intended for a particular country. We discuss the audience before choosing terminology.

Can you translate employee handbooks and training materials?

Yes. Provide the current English version, related forms and any approved terminology. For recurring updates, identify the sections that changed so the scope is clear and the Spanish materials can stay consistent.

Should we send an editable document or a PDF?

An editable source file is helpful for documents with tables, forms or frequent revisions. A complete, readable PDF can also be reviewed. Share the required final format so layout and production needs can be included in the quote.

Can you use terminology that our team has already approved?

Yes. Send your glossary, prior approved Spanish materials, product names and style preferences. Identify a contact who can resolve questions about organization-specific terms before they appear throughout a larger project.

Is certification needed for an English document translated into Spanish?

It depends on the recipient and purpose. A workplace handout and an official personal record may have different requirements. Tell us if the receiving organization asks for certification, and provide its instructions for review.

Can you support projects for offices in multiple U.S. states?

Yes. English-to-Spanish document projects can be coordinated remotely across the United States. Identify the intended readers, locations, document versions and deadlines so everyone works from the same scope.

Need an English document translated into Spanish?

Tell us the document type, audience, deadline and intended use. We’ll help you with the next step.
